# Understanding and Managing Separation Anxiety in Dogs

Does your dog bark continuously, damage household items, or become distressed whenever you leave home?

These behaviors may indicate separation-related distress. Some dogs find it difficult to remain alone, even for short periods.

## What Is Separation Anxiety?

Separation anxiety is a condition in which a dog experiences significant distress when separated from their owner or another familiar person.

It can affect dogs of different ages and breeds.

## Common Signs of Separation Anxiety

- Excessive barking or howling when left alone
- Scratching or damaging doors and furniture
- Pacing and restlessness
- Excessive drooling
- Attempts to escape
- Accidents indoors despite being house-trained

These behaviors can also have other causes. A veterinarian can help rule out medical problems.

## 5 Ways to Help Your Dog Feel Comfortable Alone

### 1. Build Independence Gradually

Encourage your dog to spend short periods comfortably away from you while you are still at home.

### 2. Create a Comfortable Resting Area

Give your dog a quiet, comfortable place with a familiar bed and safe toys.

### 3. Practice Short Departures

Begin with very brief departures that do not trigger distress. Increase the duration slowly, based on how your dog responds.

### 4. Establish a Consistent Routine

Try to maintain predictable schedules for meals, walks, playtime, and rest.

### 5. Provide Mental Enrichment

Puzzle toys and suitable food-based enrichment activities can give dogs something enjoyable to focus on.

## What Should You Avoid?

Punishing your dog for destructive behavior or barking after you return home will not address the underlying distress.

Avoid forcing your dog to remain alone for longer than they can comfortably manage.

For moderate or severe cases, work with a veterinarian and a qualified behavior professional.

## Help Your Dog Build Confidence

Learning to stay comfortable alone takes time. Progress should be gradual and based on your dog's individual comfort level.

With the right approach, you can help your dog develop healthier routines and feel more secure at home.
